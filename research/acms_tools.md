# ACMS today: the assistant's tools and what the app does

Generated from src/traffic_simulator/network_tools.py (TOOLS) for research/ledger.py's `existing` column (`ledger.py existing` writes it again).

## The assistant's 78 tools

- **network_summary**: Overview of the open network: defaults, junctions by control type, road counts, boundary ends with their flows, and the extent in metres.
- **list_roads**: Roads with their settings.
- **list_junctions**: Junctions with position (metres), control type and number of roads.
- **describe**: Everything about one junction, boundary end or road: its roads, roundabout design (NCHRP 672), signal settings, curve radius and safe curve speed, crossings.
- **analyse_network**: Design check: validity, disconnected parts, junctions traffic can't reach or leave (one-way roads considered), dead ends, very short roads, curves posted faster than their radius allows, roundabouts too big for their roads.
- **read_source**: Read the app's own source, tests or docs.
- **get_selection**: What the user has selected on the map right now.
- **show_on_map**: Select a junction, end or road on the map and centre the view on it.
- **read_traffic**: The live traffic run as it is right now (it keeps running while you read): simulated time, vehicles on the road, spawned and arrived counts, every signal's state (green, amber, red per arriving road), and each vehicle's id, type, position (x_m, y_m; y points down), heading (degrees, 0 = east, clockwise on screen), speed (km/h), road and lane or junction turn, metres along it, origin and destination.
- **list_institutions**: The institutions on the map (Demand Generator > Institutions): each one's id, kind, centre (m), area, circle of influence (its catchment), people, employees, shift, its mode shares (people % by mode; else its car share), its door (nearest junction), the junctions and ends its people come from, and its people a day each way.
- **list_vehicle_types**: The vehicle types (Mode Behavior), in order: each one's name, vehicle_class (what it is, whatever it's called: a bus calls at bus stops), timetabled (may it have a headway; a car or bike never), headway (a timetable, e.g.
- **create_vehicle_types** (changes): Add vehicle types (up to 8 in all), each made as a SUMO vehicle class with SUMO's defaults for it (size, speeds, acceleration, braking, gap): kind one of passenger, taxi, bus, coach, delivery, truck, trailer, motorcycle, moped, bicycle, tram, emergency, evehicle.
- **update_vehicle_type** (changes): Change an existing vehicle type: new_name, vehicle_class (what it is: a bus calls at bus stops; its figures stay), timetabled (true lets it have a headway; false clears it), headway_min (0-120; a timetable; only a timetabled type), occupancy (people a vehicle), length_m, width_m, max_speed_kmh, accel, decel (comfortable braking), emergency_decel (the hardest it can brake, no less than decel), min_gap_m, tau_s (reaction time), sigma (imperfection 0-1), speed_dev, pcu (passenger car units, 0.1-10), filters (in a lateral run it passes queues within its lane, if narrow).
- **read_od_matrix**: The Demand Generator's OD matrix: its origin and destination boundary ends, which demand it shows (hourly veh/h or daily trips/day: a Run uses the kind shown), every pair's hourly_vph and daily_trips (pairs with none are left out; pairs_with_data_count 0 means the matrix is empty), what most pairs share under usual (arrival pattern, split between the vehicle_types, daily curve, hourly demand's peak_hour_factor and quarters: each 15 minutes' share of the hour) and any pair that differs under different.
- **set_od_demand** (changes): Enter demand in the OD matrix the way the user does in the Demand Generator: select the pairs, type the value, pick the arrival pattern, set each vehicle type's share on the mode slider.
- **shape_daily_curve** (changes): Shape how the pairs' daily trips spread over 24 hours, in the Demand Generator's curve editor.
- **generate_demand** (changes): Fill the OD matrix by a gravity model, through the Demand Generator's controls: the trips leaving each origin end and arriving at each destination end are shared out in proportion to their supply and demand from set_end_flows (with none set anywhere: equally), sized to `total` (else the ends' own veh/h).
- **equilibrate_demand** (changes): Elastic demand (induced or suppressed traffic) after a change to the network: each OD pair's hourly demand moves towards demand × (trip time now / trip time before)^elasticity, by headless runs (iterations, default 3, of duration_min, default 30) until it settles.
- **scale_demand** (changes): Scale the demand already in the OD matrix (the kind it shows: hourly or daily), keeping its pattern between pairs: by `factor` (1.2 = 20 % more), or to `degree_of_saturation`.
- **run_simulation** (changes): Test the network as it is: one simulation, headless and as fast as the computer allows, of duration_min simulated minutes (1-240) with a seed (the Seed box's if left out) and the demand the Demand Generator shows.
- **run_experiment** (changes): Test network designs against each other: every scenario (the first is the baseline; a scenario changes the network with edit tools on a copy, scales the demand to stress it, and/or ticks extensions) runs with every seed for duration_min.
- **read_results**: Measured results: with no id, the runs and experiments of this session (ids, what they were, headline figures, workbooks) and the latest run; with a run id (R1) its figures in full (per road and per vehicle type); with an experiment id (E1) its comparison.
- **compare_runs**: Two or more runs (ids from read_results) side by side: each headline figure and its change from the first.
- **analyse_run**: Statistics of one run (an id from read_results; the latest run if left out): travel-time reliability (trip time mean, SD, CV, median, 85th and 95th percentiles; travel time, planning time and buffer indexes, network-wide and the least reliable OD pairs), each origin end's arrivals per minute against Poisson (index of dispersion, chi-square p: random, regular or bunched), and each road's 95th-percentile queue against its length (spillback where it reaches it).
- **check_calibration**: Validate a run (id; the latest if left out) against field data.
- **read_detectors**: Detector data of a run (id; the latest if left out): at the middle of each link (one direction of a road, '>' from its from-node, '<' back), flow, time-mean and space-mean speed, 15th/50th/85th percentile speeds, headways (mean, cv, shortest, share under 3 s), occupancy, density and counts a minute against Poisson; and at signalised stop lines, the measured saturation flow and start-up lost time.
- **analyse_safety**: Safety surrogate for a run (id; the latest if left out): rear-end conflicts (time to collision under 1.5 s), severe ones (TTC under 1 s or braking over 3.35 m/s² needed), per road with the rate per million vehicle-km, a safety performance function across roads (Poisson or negative binomial on flow and length) and the roads ranked by their Empirical Bayes excess (blackspots); also any collisions (same lane or crossing, by road or junction) and emergency braking.
- **forecast**: Forecast a run's series (id; the latest if left out) by Holt's exponential smoothing, fitted to it: `what` throughput, mean_speed, on_road, queued, trip_time, or a road's flow or speed (give road); interval_min (default 1), horizon (intervals ahead, default 10).
- **critical_links**: Which roads the network can least afford to lose (vulnerability): each road on a used route is closed in turn and the demand shown re-routed at free flow: the extra vehicle-hours an hour and the demand cut off, ranked; and the single points of failure (bridge roads and cut junctions: what each cuts off).
- **benefit_cost**: Appraise a scenario of an experiment (id) against its baseline in money: person-hours of delay, vehicle-km and car CO2 saved per simulated hour, valued (value_of_time_per_h, cost_per_veh_km, carbon_per_tonne), over hours_per_year and years (default 30); NPV and benefit-cost ratio at discount_rates_percent (default 3.5, 4, 7) against capital_cost (and annual_cost); the BCR's range over the seeds and whether the decision flips with the rate.
- **design_audit**: Check the network's design against the AASHTO Green Book and a road safety checklist: sight distance on curves and at junctions without signals (against the placed buildings), turns too tight for the largest vehicle type, junction legs meeting under 75 degrees, arterial junctions under 250 m apart, lane balance at merges and diverges; each finding with the rule.
- **accessibility_report**: What people can reach (Levine; Hansen): from each junction, jobs, school places, hospitals and shops within budgets_min (default [15, 30]) and potential access to jobs (exp(-beta_per_min x minutes), default 0.1); for the residents (Residential buildings) the means, the least served and how evenly it's shared (Gini).
- **regress**: Least-squares regression with t-tests, R² and F: `y` on `x` (names) over `data` (a list of objects) or over an experiment's runs (`experiment` id: each run's figures, keyed as in its comparison, plus demand_scale).
- **junction_los**: Level of service per junction and approach for a run (id; the latest if left out): control delay (each vehicle's time on the approach and through the junction less that at free flow), graded A-F by the HCM 2000 (signals Exhibit 16-2; give-way, stop and roundabout entries 17-2), signal approaches' v/c, 95th-percentile queues against their road, and LOS per 15-minute period.
- **diagnose_signal**: Why a signal performs as it does in a run (id; the latest if left out), each signalised junction or the one given: flow ratios, critical v/c and Webster's cycle for the demand, per approach v/c, arrivals on green and arrival type (progression), the HCM delay against the simulated delay, spillback; then the likely causes ranked, each with the tool that fixes it.
- **check_run_health**: Can a run (id; the latest if left out) be trusted? Gridlock, vehicles that couldn't enter, OD pairs with no route, most trips unfinished at the end, a warm-up of half the run, signal approaches over capacity; each problem with what to do (FHWA Toolbox III error checking).
- **preflight_check** (changes): Check the network before trusting tests (FHWA Toolbox III): a run at a tenth of the demand listing links driven far slower than free flow with no signal to explain it (a fault in the network), and a run at half the demand checked for gridlock and blocked entries.
- **resilience**: A disrupted run against the same seed's undisrupted run (both ids): minute by minute, when the disruption starts, the biggest drop, minutes to recover, performance kept and extra vehicle-hours (the resilience triangle).
- **emissions_report**: Fuel and CO2 of a run's cars and vans (id; the latest if left out) by Biggs and Akcelik's power model from each vehicle's speed and acceleration: totals, per type, per vehicle-km and person-km, and the roads burning most.
- **power_analysis**: How many seeds a comparison needs (paired t-test).
- **test_agent_compliance** (changes): Test an agent-decided extension (TSP or DEVL, as its tab chooses the model) on a fixed battery: the network as it is, 30 % more demand, heavy rain and a lane closed, each on `seeds` (default 1, 2) for duration_min (default 10); with DEVL, emergency vehicles every 4 minutes.
- **read_decisions**: The agent's decisions in a run (id; the latest if left out), TSP's and DEVL's: when asked, the junction, who decided, the answer, latency, the outcome (served, not needed, blocked: a hindsight check), the telemetry it read and the person-delay estimate.
- **decision_sensitivity** (changes): What the TSP agent relies on: a sample (default 8) of a run's TSP decisions re-asked to the model deciding now, each with one fact changed: no_passengers, exit_full, no_cross_queues, arrives_on_green, no_estimate; how often each flips the answer, and how often the same question asked again keeps it.
- **rank_scenarios**: Rank every scenario of an experiment (id) on one figure (as in its comparison), all pairs, not just against the baseline: randomized-block ANOVA and Friedman's rank test (no normality needed), every pair's paired difference (Bonferroni) with Wilcoxon's p, and group letters: scenarios sharing a letter aren't told apart.
- **network_indices**: Graph indices of the road network (Geography of Transport Systems): vertices, edges, components, cyclomatic number, alpha, beta, gamma, the diameter in links, degrees, the detour index between boundary ends (route km over straight km) and the most and least accessible junctions (Shimbel).
- **cluster_conditions**: Group observed days into travel conditions by k-means (FHWA Toolbox III 2019): `days` [{"label": "Mon 3 Mar", "values": {"volume": 5200, "rain_mm": 0, "incidents": 1}}, ...], each attribute normalised 0-1; k chosen by the within/between variance ratio unless given.
- **build_scenario** (changes): Build and test what the user describes in one go (the Scenario Builder): fill in only what they say; the app adds stated defaults, plans the steps (network from a place or a grid, junction control, signal timing, buses, demand sized to a traffic level and checked in a run, mode choice, route choice, a baseline run with its statistics, the reports asked for, and each what-if as a paired experiment against the network as built), shows the user the plan to apply once, runs it as one undo step and returns a plain report with the measured numbers.
- **assign_routes** (changes): Route choice for every run that follows (it is saved with the network): each OD pair gets up to `routes` alternative routes (default 3) and each vehicle draws one by path-size logit on link times, theta_per_min (default 0.5: higher, more to the fastest).
- **optimise_signals** (changes): Search fixed-time signal settings for `junctions` (signalised) by a genetic algorithm, each candidate's cycle and green split scored by headless runs on the same seeds (default 1-3): objective delay (default), trip_delay, throughput or conflicts; population (4-10, default 6) x generations (1-5, default 3), at most 120 runs.
- **set_extension** (changes): Tick or untick an extension in the Extensions tab by name (e.g.
- **read_extensions**: Every extension in the Extensions tab: whether it is ticked, what it does, and each setting set_extension can fill, by label, with its value and what it can be (e.g.
- **schedule_emergency** (changes): Replace the Dispatch Emergency Vehicle tab's schedules: each sends an emergency vehicle from one place to another (a boundary end like 1W or a junction like 2,1, with a route between them), every every_min minutes from the start (0: once, at once) or once after_min minutes in (a schedule has one of the two).
- **start_batch** (changes): Start Batch Run: every seed (like "1-4" or "3, 7, 9") runs duration_min simulated minutes, headless, against the baselines (never, always preempt).
- **replay_run** (changes): Run a batch run's workbook (path) again from its telemetry feed, each decision as recorded, no model asked, and say whether it came out the same.
- **calibrate_demand** (changes): Fit the hourly OD matrix to counted flows: `counts` [{"road": id, "vph": n}] (both directions of a two-way road together).
- **choose_modes** (changes): Set each OD pair's split between vehicle types by mode choice (multinomial logit) instead of a fixed split: per pair, each alternative's utility = constant + in_vehicle_per_min x in-vehicle minutes + out_of_vehicle_per_min x (out_of_vehicle_min + half its headway) + cost x (cost + cost_per_km x route km).
- **find_tools**: More tools: you are offered the ones this request most likely needs.
- **note_plan**: Write down, before testing, what you plan and why: the goal, your hypothesis, the design changes you'll test and what you expect to see.
- **set_signal_timing** (changes): Set how signalised junctions are timed: method one of webster, transyt, synchro, scats, scoot, actuated, max_pressure, fixed (webster: each junction alone from its demand; transyt, synchro: coordinated offline; scats, scoot: adaptive from detectors; actuated: each green extends while vehicles keep coming, 7-50 s; max_pressure: the phase with the most vehicles waiting, less those queued past it, goes next; fixed: the green_times_s given), phases 2 or 4, green_times_s (one a phase, for fixed), cycle_min_s and cycle_max_s (20-240, the coordinated methods), target_saturation_percent (50-110, SCATS and SCOOT), stop_weight_s (TRANSYT and SCOOT).
- **place_map** (changes): Put OpenStreetMap under the network: a `place` found by name (Nominatim; the first match), or lat and lon; rotation_deg turns the map.
- **add_bus_stop** (changes): Add a bus stop (a bay beside the kerb) on a road: direction forward (from -> to), backward or both; dwell_s how long buses stand (20 by default).
- **update_bus_stop** (changes): Move a bus stop along its road (distance_m from the road's from-node), change the traffic it serves, its dwell time, or its passengers (boarding_per_h, alighting_percent; both 0: none).
- **update_institution** (changes): Change a placed institution's figures: people, employees, shift_start_h and shift_end_h (hours after midnight), car_share (percent; replaces a mode split: Car that share, the rest walk), radius_m (its circle of influence: the catchment its people come from).
- **set_junction_control** (changes): Make junctions signalised, roundabouts (sized from their roads) or uncontrolled.
- **update_roads** (changes): Change road settings; give only what changes.
- **add_junction** (changes): Add an off-grid junction (named N1, N2, ...).
- **add_road** (changes): Connect two junctions with a new two-way road.
- **add_boundary_end** (changes): Add a boundary end at (x, y) joined to a junction; named by side and position, e.g.
- **move_junctions** (changes): Move junctions or ends by (dx, dy) metres; only their roads stretch.
- **delete_objects** (changes): Delete roads, junctions (with their roads), boundary ends, bus stops or institutions.
- **build_interchange** (changes): Build a grade-separated interchange.
- **add_slip_road** (changes): Add a one-way slip road past a junction for the kerb-side turn (left when driving on the left) between 2 roads meeting there, in either order.
- **set_end_flows** (changes): Set a boundary end's supply (entering) and/or demand (leaving) in veh/h, one end a call.
- **place_institution** (changes): Place one institution on clear ground: near a junction or end (the nearest clear spot to it) or at x, y (m).
- **institution_mode_split** (changes): Each institution's mode split by multinomial logit (the Institutions panel's Mode split): its people choose between Walk and the vehicle types for the trips from each junction or end in its circle to its door (route km).
- **deploy_institutions** (changes): Place institutions at random on clear ground within the network's extent (never on a road, a junction or another building), each with its kind's usual figures and a circle of influence (the catchment its people come from) reaching its 4 nearest junctions or ends (or radius_m).
- **network_from_background** (changes): Replace the whole network (undoable) with the roads of the background.
- **generate_grid** (changes): Replace the whole network with a new grid (undoable).
