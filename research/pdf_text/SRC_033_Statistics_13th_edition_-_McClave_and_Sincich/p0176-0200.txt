  In Section 3.5, we showed that the probability of event A may be substantially
  altered by the knowledge that an event B has occurred. However, this will not always
  be the case; in some instances, the assumption that event B has occurred will not alter
  the probability of event A at all. When this occurs, we say that the two events A and B
  are independent events.

  Events A and B are independent events if the occurrence of B does not alter the
  probability that A has occurred; that is, events A and B are independent if

  P1A ͉ B2 = P1A2

  When events A and B are independent, it is also true that
  P1B ͉ A2 = P1B2

  Events that are not independent are said to be dependent.
