1.2.1 Components of ITS

Smart roads, smart vehicles, and smart parking are the primary components of
smart transportation. Figure 1.2 illustrates the components of smart transportation.
Smart roads use sensors and actuators to improve driving safety and reduce
environmental impact. Real-time computing and communication skills are used by
automated cars to interact with and advance social well-being. Smart parking helps
drivers find a parking spot that is suitable for their car by using an automated
method. The raw data collected from these components at the base station is pro-
cessed and analysed to improve the efficiency of road decision-making. When
combined, these three components provide an accurate and intricate transportation
model. With this system, drivers and customers may receive real-time transporta-
tion information on things like traffic, weather, parking availability, and road
safety. Reduced accident risk, better safety, reduced fuel consumption, and
increased comfort are just a few of the numerous benefits of an effective smart
transportation paradigm. Information about traffic, weather, parking availability,
and road safety may be made available to drivers and customers via a real-time
system. An effective intelligent transportation system reduces the likelihood of
mishaps, enhances safety, consumes less fuel, and improves passenger comfort [7].

1.2.2 Standards for smart transportation system

Numerous technologies that enable communication in automobiles have surfaced in
recent years. All the technologies used on a national, interstate, and international

the simultaneous emergence of a global push for the establishment of ITS. Several

  The establishment of 204 technical committees for ITS by the International
Organization for Standardization (ISO) in 1992 marked the beginning of ITS

information systems, interaction systems, and land transportation in both urban
and rural areas. The European Committee for Standardization (CEN) initiated
the development of ITS standards in Europe in 1992. The European
Telecommunication Standards Institute (ETSI), a division of CEN, has more
members. Another ISO-recognised standard organisation is the International
Telecommunication Union (ITU). International standards for ITS are being devel-
oped by the ITU and ISO together. For use in car communication networks, the
Institute of Electrical and Electronics Engineers (IEEE) has created several

1.3 The main development projects of smart
  transportation

This section presents some real-time projects implemented and analysed [9].

l Advanced Traffic Management Services (ATMS): The intelligent transpor-
  tation system is built on ATMs. The traffic monitoring system transfers
  traffic conditions it observes to the traffic control centre over a network
  using technologies including detection, communication, and control. The
  traffic control centre gathers and combines data from several sources.
  Following analysis, suitable traffic control and management measures are
  applied based on the findings to maximise transportation efficiency and
  safety.

l Advanced Traveller Information Services (ATIS): ATIS employs modern
  information, communication, and other associated technologies to give tra-
  vellers pre-trip information, route advice, and real-time information while
  travelling.

l Advanced Public Transportation Services (APTS): APTS integrates ATMS,
  ATIS, and Advanced Vehicle Control and Safety Systems (AVCSS) services
  into the public transportation system to improve service quality, operational
  efficiency, and public transport utilisation.

l Commercial Vehicle Operation Services (CVOS): CVOS employs ATMS,
  ATIS, and AVCSS for commercial vehicles to increase transportation effi-
  ciency and safety while lowering labour costs.

l Electronic Payment Services (EPS): EPS is an application of information and
  communication integration technology that allows customers to utilise a stan-
  dard and easy payment medium for payment behaviour in automobiles, saving
  them not only travel time but also the need to carry cash.

l Emergency Management Service (EMS): EMS is a service that directs rescue
  vehicles to the area as quickly as possible and alerts other drivers when an
  emergency occurs.

l Vulnerable Individual Protection Service (VIPS): VIPS is based on traffic-
  vulnerable users, such as pedestrians, children, the elderly, and persons with
  disabilities, and uses applicable facilities and equipment or management
  approaches to improve traffic safety and meet their service needs.

l Information Management Service (IMS): IMS offers data gathering, archiving,
  administration, and application services via the implementation of an ITS-
  related data file management system.

1.4 AIoT technologies for smart transportation systems

Various frameworks have been adopted for developing smart transportation such as
distributed computing, cloud computing, edge cloud computing, and smart trans-
portation communication protocols shown in Figure 1.4 [10–12].

  Distributed computing: Cloud computing and other forms of centralised
computing have greatly aided and generated smart transportation applications.
Because of the sensitivity to latency and large amounts of transportation data, many
applications in the field of transportation require distributed data processing instead
of centralised data processing. For instance, to escape traffic congestion, drivers in
urban areas sometimes must make snap judgements on whether to shift lanes or
take alternate routes. To aid in decision-making, an app must collect crucial data
like location, vehicle speeds, traffic patterns, and collisions. It must also immedi-
ately analyse this data and respond. The requirement for the cloud infrastructure to
collect and analyse copious data in a short period is a hurdle in this scenario. This
load on the cloud is greatly reduced while maintaining fast response times when a
distributed data processing architecture is used.

  Centralised computing: Smart transport systems have been developed by
several researchers using cutting-edge technologies. Because of its sophisticated
electronic data storage and transfer features, cloud computing is an important
player. With the least amount of work, equipment, and upfront expenses, it allows
us to develop and implement computing services. To handle big datasets generated
by ITS, cloud computing provides centralised and extensible processing and sto-
rage capacity. Predictive analytics about safety and other advanced data analytics
are handled by cloud servers, which also help to improve the system’s overall
intelligence.

1.4.1 IoTs place in Intelligent Transportation Systems

Novel applications for smart transport are emerging because of developments in big
data, cloud computing, wireless sensor networking, and the IoT. The IoT is a collection
of physical objects with sensors and internet connections, processors, and commu-
nication technology installed to collect data from their environment. These gadgets
provide broad monitoring platforms that enable massive data collecting and real-time
data sharing, which serves as the cornerstone of ITS. IoT stands for the integration of
data from many items utilising pre-existing internet infrastructure onto a virtual plat-
form. Any device that has an on/off switch and an internet connection is considered an
IoT device [13,14].

  Numerous facets of smart transportation, including intelligent mobility, smart
traffic management, and effective parking solutions, have seen substantial
advancements thanks to IoT applications. With the help of these advancements,
smart transportation systems can now offer drivers the best routes, prompt parking
reservations, energy-efficient street lighting, telematics for public transportation,
accident prevention, and the use of sensors in vehicles, mobile devices, and city
infrastructure to allow remote driving [8,13,15,16].

1.4.3 AIs place in the Internet of Things

AI enhances the capabilities of the IoT technological ecosystem, allowing for more
intelligent, efficient, and data-driven applications. Here are some major roles for AI
in IoT in smart transportation [19–21]:

l Analysing and processing the data: IoT devices produce vast volumes of data.
  In real-time, AI can collect and analyse the data, identifying trends, irregula-
  rities, and patterns that humans would find challenging to notice.

l Operative forecasting: AI can utilise previous IoT data to forecast future
  occurrences or patterns. For example, it may forecast equipment breakdowns,
  maintenance requirements, and even customer behaviour.

l Computerisation and Digitisation: Artificial intelligence may be utilised to
  develop self-contained systems capable of controlling and optimising IoT
  devices and processes. This is especially beneficial in industrial IoT applica-
  tions, where robots and systems must adapt and make choices without human
  interaction.

l Detection of anomalies: AI algorithms can discover unexpected patterns in IoT
  data, which is crucial for spotting security vulnerabilities or abnormal beha-
  viour in connected devices. It facilitates proactive security measures.

l Natural Language Processing (NLP): Combining NLP and IoT empowers
  devices to comprehend and respond to human language, making voice-
  activated smart assistants a popular application.

l Customisation: Using IoT data, AI may analyse user behaviour and pre-
  ferences to provide personalised experiences such as tailored content, sug-
  gestions, and settings in smart homes and wearable devices.

l Efficiency in energy use: AI can optimise energy use in IoT applications like
  smart buildings and smart grids by modifying systems based on real-time data,
  weather conditions, and human activity.

l Health and Social Services: IoT sensors and wearables can track health para-
  meters, and AI may use that information to give early detection, health advice,
  and remote patient monitoring.

1.8 Conclusion

This study identified critical categories in which AI may help advance and operate
self-driving cars, intelligent traffic control, and improve safety measures. IoT tech-
nology enhances the effect by allowing for Vehicle-to-Everything (V2X) commu-
nication, predictive maintenance, and energy management. The combination of AI
and IoT has transformative potential for transportation services, improving efficacy,
safety, and sustainability. To realise the potential of new technologies, policymakers
and urban planners must adopt frameworks that can accommodate them, encourage
innovation, and ensure that benefits are dispersed equally. An in-depth view of
applications and current technologies (distributed computing, centralised computing,
and edge cloud computing) used to develop smart transportation is mentioned. Also,
the journey of smart transportation is highlighted from no technology to autonomous
transport. Smart roads, smart parking, and smart vehicles are the main components of
smart transportation. Better ability, environmental friendliness, and ease of access are
some of its benefits illustrated. Tehran, parts of India, Barcelona, and more places
have implemented this transportation service as an example of smart roads and
parking, as well as an example of smart vehicles “Ford” applied the AIoT in their
cars. Although a smart transportation system has a lot of benefits, execution of full
functions is still difficult. So, implementation has become a big challenge these days.

Abstract

Artificial Intelligence of Things stands for the revolutionary combination of Artificial
Intelligence and the Internet of Things, transforming sectors by merging powerful AI
capabilities into smart, connected devices. With this synergy, IoT systems will be
significantly revolutionised in efficiency, intelligence, and adaptability, unlocking a
wide range of transformative innovations. These technologies change the world by
processing information in real time, predicting, and making decisions that are more
intelligent and efficient. In this context, this chapter discusses AIoTs role in modern
transportation systems. Relating to the two strong pillars of AI and IoT, the chapter
illustrates how they are better interlinked for practical application in sustainable
transport solutions. The core technological concepts and engineering developments
underpinning AIoT systems are described, focusing on their operational models and
frameworks. It further elaborates on adopting AIoT in the transportation sector,
including traffic management, autonomous and connected vehicles, fleet manage-
ment, and predictive maintenance. Then, the case studies and real-world applications
are presented at various levels of their practical implementation to motivate success
stories and describe the importance of AIoT in transportation, along with potential
benefits and challenges. By implementing AIoT methodologies, the world is not only
enhancing technological capabilities but also laying the foundation for a more
intelligent and interconnected future where smart systems unlock unprecedented
levels of efficiency and innovation.

2.1 Introduction to AIoT technologies

A combination of artificial intelligence with IoT in today’s fast-moving tech-
nological world has formed a game-changing phenomenon that finds broad
implications in diverse industries. This integration marks an important change in
how intelligent systems, now referred to as AIoT, connect with and adapt to the
physical world. By integrating IoTs big data collecting and networking cap-
abilities with the advanced analytical and decision-making powers of AI, AIoT
will drive a new class of technologies that are not only much more perceptive
and adaptive but also capable of autonomous decision-making. All this integra-
tion promotes application efficiency and functionality, enabling real-time insight
and action.

2.1.1 Definition and concept of AIoT

Are IoT and AI related? How, where, and where do they meet? If AI is considered
the intelligent ‘brain’ of something, then IoT would be the ‘digital nervous system’.
Information systems present remarkable synergies when combined. In practice,
combining AI with IoT allows connected devices to analyse the data collected. It
then acts on them without any human intervention [1].

2.1.1.1 Understanding AI
In simple words, AI is the simulation or replication of human intelligence in
machines [1]. This includes data analysis, pattern recognition, decision-making,
problem-solving, and many others. AI comprises various types of technologies,
such as machine learning, deep learning, Natural Language Processing (NLP), and
computer vision. As seen in Figure 2.1, the key components of AI are (i) Machine
Learning: Algorithms that enable systems to learn and improve via experience; (ii)
Deep Learning refers to advanced machine learning techniques using neural net-
works; (iii) NLP helps robots understand and respond to human language; (iv)
Computer vision enables machines to analyse and make decisions based on visual
data.

2.1.1.2 Understanding IoT
Though it is not yet clear whether machines have finally achieved the power to
equal human problem-solving capabilities, it has certainly expanded the limits of
human senses. These everyday machines constitute what is referred to as the
Internet of Things, or IoT. In simple words, IoT is the growing network of devices,
vehicles, appliances, and other stuff embedded with electronics, software, and
sensors that connect and exchange data over the internet. The very basic purpose of
IoT is to enable these gadgets to communicate and interact with each other in a
smarter, more interconnected world. As seen in Figure 2.2, the key components of
IoT are (i) Devices/Sensors that capture data from the environment; (ii)
Connectivity: It enables the devices to communicate with one another through
networks (Wi-Fi, Bluetooth, cellular, etc.); (iii) Data Processing: Analyses data that

2.1.2 AIoT: Evolution and convergence

The evolution of AIoT, as seen in Figure 2.3, has been a journey from device-level IoT
to intelligent systems-level IoT. Traditional devices in the early days of IoT (1980s–
1990s) worked in an isolated fashion, collecting data on their own without talking to
each other; that is what is referred to as Siloed IoT. During these days of isolated data
collection, devices such as telematics and environmental sensors worked in absolute
isolation from each other. With advancements in technology into the 2000s, these
devices got connected through IoT and were allowed to share data by communicating
with other devices to break these silos. In this phase, applications were getting wider,
and integration of data was better, furthering the possibility of holistic solutions.

  The introduction of augmented IoT during the 2010s introduced analytics into
the picture-discussed turning raw data into actionable insights. This transformation
allowed devices not only to collect and share data but also to analyse it for pre-
dictive maintenance, optimised performance, and decision-making. In the late
2010s came AIoT, where AI itself is integrated into IoT systems, enabling auton-
omous operations; this stage allowed systems themselves to analyse data and make
decisions independently, such as route optimisation in telematics or environmental
condition control automatically.

2.2 Literature survey

Traffic congestion, a significant issue in developing as well as developed countries,
arises due to a variety of reasons. Therefore, various studies have explored different
approaches to address this issue. Recent developments in technology have sig-
nificantly improved traffic management systems due to the incorporation of AI
and the IoT. As mentioned by different researchers, AIoT technologies are key
enablers of accurate traffic predictions. They have improved traffic flow rate, as
well as reduced road congestion.

  Ata provides an artificial intelligence system-based (AI) congestion prediction
based on traffic. This proposed algorithm is called Modelling smart road traffic con-
gestion Regulation using neural network artificial backpropagation (MSR2C-
ABPNN). The programme predicts the congestion and also provides alternate routes
as displayed on vehicle LCDs similar to Google Maps [3]. To identify traffic delays, a
model called Artificial Neural Networks (ANNs), fused with fuzzy logic, was used.
The model included time, distance, wind speed, temperature, and humidity as the key
parameters with which traffic delay could be estimated. The simulation results
demonstrate that it is indeed possible to generate expert functions provided by a well-
trained network to the data for training [4].

  Li presented the idea of predicting traffic accidents based on deep learning
machines. Eight metrics comprising: accident description, incident occurrence,
upstream station speed (in kilometres/hour), upstream occupancy (percentage),
upstream capacity (vehicles per lane/hour), downstream speed (in kilometres/hour),
downstream occupancy (percentage) and downstream capacity (vehicles per lane/hour)
were used to obtain data from California on traffic incidents. It then involved the
implementation of an extreme learning machine (ELM) which was compared with
naive Bayes and support vector machines [5]. Ma suggested a transportation conges-
tion forecasting system utilising deep learning methods. The data on transport network

congestion is collected by Global Positioning System (GPS). GPS tracks the position,
time, and pace of connections. The neural network receives the reported data as
feedback and produces congestion forecasts. The prediction uses RNN-RBM
(Recurrent Neural Network–Restricted Boltzmann Machine) algorithms [6].

  Sakran investigated a network architecture containing IoT agent technology that is

mework comprising countless heterogeneous devices. The networks include various
industrial practices with active radio frequency identification (RFID), wireless sensor

  Mishu has designed a real-time system that helps in monitoring and managing
road traffic. IoT is used as a cloud with storage servers and applications. Through
the RF transmitter, the emergency traffic control is carried out. When cars move
over the load cells, they turn the loading action into electrical pulses. Akinboro
illustrated smart road traffic monitoring networks that use weighted cameras, GPS,
very small aperture terminal (VSAT), control rooms, and hand-held computers. To
get the information on the car, the sensors carrying the weighted plates are mounted
to the roadside, and then the information is sent to the control room. If the col-
lective traffic exceeds the transportation limit, the device sends a text alert message
for the congested road and suggests a viable route for road users [7].

  Sadhukhan has proposed a device that is IoT-enabled and includes two parts, a
traffic density management module (TDMM) and a traffic management module
(TMM). The device’s main function is to monitor the number of vehicles and to
change signal lights depending on the road traffic situation. The sensor, installed on the
roadside, is the TDMM that uses this technology to measure the traffic density of the
road. The data then is sent to TMM, the traffic lights control, so that it will change the
traffic lights or maintain them based on the density levels at the road intersection [8].

2.3.2.1 Data collection
AIoT heavily relies on Internet of Things devices containing particular sensors
continuously bringing data from the environment. These sensors can include
cameras, GPS modules, temperature sensors, and motion detectors. The collected
data serves as the raw input for AIoT systems, which rely on these sensors for real-
time insights and decision-making capabilities. Connectivity is a critical compo-
nent, which transfers data to central systems or cloud platforms for further pro-
cessing, making data collection efficient across various applications [10].

2.3.2.2 Data transmission
Upon gathering data, IoT devices would subsequently need to transmit data effec-
tively for analysis and processing. It may use a variety of communication protocols
such as Wi-Fi, Bluetooth, Zigbee, LoRa, and cellular networks, including 4G and 5G
to send out data efficiently. The protocols are selected based on a series of con-
siderations, including range, data rate, power consumption, and specific applications.
Data is processed locally on the edge. This method is referred to as Edge Computing.
This decreases latency and bandwidth for all real-time applications [11].

2.3.2.3 Data processing
The processing of data in AIoT systems generally involves the integration of AI
models that analyse and make insights from the data gathered. Edge AI is the
  Understanding AIoT technologies and their relevance 25

technology that enables the implementation of AI models directly on edge devices, such
as smart cameras or sensors, which can directly process data where it originates and
offer real-time responses independently of cloud connectivity [11]. On the other hand,
Cloud AI deals more with complex data analysis, machine learning model training, and
mass data processing. Together, Edge AI and Cloud AI create a strong data processing
framework that balances speed, efficiency, and computational power [11].

2.3.2.4 Decision making
AIoT systems also need to incorporate advanced AI algorithms and machine
learning models to interpret data to arrive at intelligent decision-making. These
models are trained to run on neural networks, decision trees, and other machine
learning algorithms to analyse several data points from a complex dataset, find
patterns, and create actionable insights [11]. A good example is in the Smart
Transportation Systems (STS) where AI models analyse traffic data to predict
congestion and suggest alternative routes that would optimise traffic flow.

2.3.2.5 Feedback loop
Feedback loops are an essential feature of AIoT systems that allow for continuous
learning and optimisation. The outcomes will constantly be monitored and fed back
into the system. These feedbacks cause AI models to learn from past experiences,
adjust parameters, and, in turn, improve on future performances. This will make the
AIoT systems iteratively learn, and be more adaptable and responsive.

2.3.2.6 User interaction and interface
Various interfaces for user interaction are provided by the AIoT system making it
simple and easy to use. Often used for this purpose are user dashboards as well as
alerts that will show several things like data insights, and system status among
others, and also provide actionable recommendations [11]. Mobile apps or dedi-
cated control panels are common places where you can access these interfaces
allowing users to monitor and manage AIoT systems all over the world.

2.4.1 Smart traffic management systems

STS play an important role in mitigating traffic congestion in urban areas. In recent
years, traffic congestion has been recognised as one of the major social problems
within urban settings around the globe. This poses considerable challenges to trans-
portation systems, the economy, and the personal satisfaction of residents [13]. One
of the Innovative Transportation Systems (also known as STS) has been considered
one of the feasible ways to solve the significant problem of congestion that has led to
the change of transportation and therefore urban areas. STS brings all the technolo-
gies such as the IoT, remote sensor organisations, information analysis, and intelli-
gent dynamic systems together to create an intelligent transportation system [13].
The system operates in a decentralised manner by processing data retrieved from the
nodes and processing the data videos at local servers and then assesses the cumula-
tive occupancy or density to assist with density-based traffic signal control. What the
system does additionally is it reacts to emergency vehicles, i.e., ambulances and fire
brigades. Moreover, it lets users know when a road is jammed through prediction.
