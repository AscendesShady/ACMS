9.5 AIoT technologies driving smart tourism

AIoT technologies are transforming the way tourists experience destinations,
making travel smarter, more responsive, and personalised. The emerging power
combinations of IoT sensors, AI-based decision-making processes, AR/VR, digital
twin, and wearable technology transform tourist experiences and provide
seamlessness-enriched travel. These innovations enable cities and regions to be
better able to respond to the changing expectations of tourists. They provide real-
time information, individualised guidance, and interactive navigation for tourists.

l IoT Sensors and Real-Time Data Collection: The IoT sensors form the basis
  of AIoT in intelligent tourism. These sensors collect vast amounts of data based
  on transportation networks and from tourist hotspots. They are distributed in
  cities, vehicles, and infrastructure to constantly monitor and sense the flow of
  traffic, weather, air quality, vehicle availability, foot traffic, etc. Through such
  real-time data, informed and adaptive decisions are made to directly influence
  tourist experiences. A simple example of this is through IoT sensors placed
  inside buses or trains, capturing occupancies and sharing them with visitors to
  choose less crowded alternatives. Similarly, by installing sensors in a tourist
  destination, the level of congestion can be known, thus alternatives can be pre-
  sented to discourage crowding. Capabilities for real-time data gathering will
  allow city planners and tourism operators to develop an atmosphere that is well-
  balanced for the tourist hence making it easy and fluid at a trip.

l AI-Based Decision Support Systems for Tourists: Through using AI, the
  information collected by IoT sensors is processed by machine learning algo-
  rithms to provide actionable recommendations for the tourist. These systems
  scan data patterns and forecast tourist needs, offering real-time recommenda-
  tions that can enhance the tourist experience. For example, AI systems could
  predict the peak hours that tourists are likely to visit a site and alert them or
  suggest visiting during less busy hours. These decision support systems can
  even recommend customised itineraries according to a tourist’s preferences,
  past behaviour, or real-time conditions. For example, an art enthusiast tourist
  might be recommended the most relevant museums, galleries, or street arts
  publicly on view, along with travel route advice and the best time to visit.
  Recommendations from AI can be tailored according to segment, making the
  experience for tourists more relevant, enjoyable, and overall spread across
  the city.

l AR/VR and Digital Twin Technology in Transportation: AR, VR, and other
  digital twin technologies create highly immersive experiences for tourists,
  enhancing navigation and spatial exploration. In fact, digital twins refer to
  virtual replicas of physical and real-world spaces, which would be used to
  showcase simulated transportation routes, stations, and landmarks to offer
  virtual previews of any destination on the cards. Digital twins enable cities to
  monitor and optimise transportation networks while simultaneously providing
  tourists with information about transit and attractions before their visit. AR, for
  Enhancing tourists’ experience with AIoT for Industry 5.0 187

  example, allows for better navigation because it superimposes directions, real-
  time data, or historical information onto physical landmarks using a smart-
  phone or other wearables. For instance, an application may utilise AR to
  instruct a tourist on how to navigate a city by displaying visual hints or his-
  torical facts on the way to a notable landmark. On the other hand, VR allows
  tourists to interact with areas, experience destinations, or preview a tour before
  travelling. Both AR and VR make the journey interactive and educational,
  creating great memories that connect the tourist with the surroundings.
l Mobile Applications and Wearable Technology for Tourists: Mobile
  applications and wearable devices are must-haves for each traveller in modern
  times as they allow easy interaction with AIoT-driven transportation systems.
  The tourist will be updated in real time through interactive maps and tailored
  recommendations based on his or her needs as a personal guide through the
  destination. Many smart tourism applications also allow tourists to book tran-
  sit, receive alerts for their commute, and access virtual concierge services with
  information on what is available at local attractions, where to dine, or what to
  see and do. Wearable technology can even amplify that: convenient, hands-
  free interaction with the transportation network. A smartwatch or AR glasses
  can provide navigation cues, alert them to near points of interest, or digitally
  let them access tickets. Some wearables are equipped with a number of health
  monitoring sensors making them quite useful to elderly or differently-abled
  tourists who could receive alert messages about emergencies or need for
  medical assistance. In this regard, wearables and mobile applications facilitate
  easier navigation by tourists to unfamiliar destinations while remaining
  informed and connected.

AIoT technologies like the sensors integrated into IoT, AI decision support, AR/
VR, digital twins, mobile applications, and wearables change the tourism experi-
ence. AIoT enables a tourist to explore destinations much better through real-time
data, intelligent recommendations, and immersive interaction with the destination.
Thus, these technologies will improve transportation, simplify travel, and create
memorable experiences for today’s increasingly common smart travellers.

9.7 Challenges and considerations

Despite the huge potential of AIoT in smart tourism, it is riddled with a myriad of
challenges and considerations requiring responsible and effective application.
These range from privacy and data security concerns, ethical implications, and
technical and infrastructure-related difficulties to navigating regulatory frame-
works. Each of these is important to ensure that AIoT systems benefit tourists,
operators, and society without taking unnecessary risks.

l Issues Concerning Privacy and Data Security: Another critical issue in smart
  tourism with AIoT pertains to handling tourist personal data. AIoT is wired to
  collect real-time information from various devices, including mobile, wearable
  sensors, and public infrastructure, which will collect sensitive information, such
  as the location, history of travel, or preferences of a place. This information is
  critical in developing customised and responsive tourism activities but also
  poses risks to privacy if not managed safely. Data security is equally important
  because the breach may compromise all the sensitive information of tourists.
  Cybersecurity measures like encryption, secure data storage, and robust access
  control protocols are necessary to safeguard data from unauthorised access or
  hacking. In addition, observance of such privacy laws as GDPR or CCPA is also
  crucial since such laws stipulate well-defined policies regarding the collection,
  storage, and consent of data. Tourism operators must be transparent with the
  tourists by telling them what data is being collected and how it will be used, and
  allowing them to opt out in case they want to.

l Ethical Dimensions of AIoT in Tourism: Ethical dimensions in deploying
  AIoT are quite complex and go beyond issues of privacy. Decisions about
  routes, services, or personal recommendations to other tourists may be biased
  in a way that runs the experiences of others based on certain demographics.
  Past behaviour determines different recommendations from certain demo-
  graphics, thus limiting their experiences. The considerations of fairness and
  inclusivity are important in avoiding unintentional forms of discrimination in
  AI-based decision-making. However, there are ethical concerns regarding the
  level of surveillance AIoT-based tourism systems would deploy. At this point,
  safety and individual freedom seem to rub shoulders in what seems easily seen
  as a thin line, and this makes the observer ethically anxious. Careful policy
  planning, transparency, and stakeholder involvement are also very important
  considerations in making AIoT solutions that will prioritise autonomy, equal-
  ity, and fairness for tourists.
  Enhancing tourists’ experience with AIoT for Industry 5.0 191

l Technological and Infrastructure Barriers in Implementation: Implementing
  AIoT in tourism infrastructure involves a number of technical and logistical diffi-
  culties. Creating and sustaining a web of sensors, devices, and software is very
  resource-intensive in terms of hardware and skilled labour. Many destinations in
  the tourism sector simply do not possess the infrastructure and technological
  capacity to deploy and maintain such systems, especially in areas with low budgets
  or limited access to modern technology. Furthermore, AIoT systems also need to
  handle large amounts of data in real time. Hence, the demand for strong data
  processing capabilities and high reliability in connectivity is a must. It is difficult to
  maintain an AIoT infrastructure in areas with either limited internet connectivity or
  inconsistent power supply. Another consideration comes in line with compatibility
  among various devices and technologies because often multiple vendors and plat-
  forms are involved in an AIoT system. All these technical and infrastructure issues
  require strategic planning, a significant investment of resources, and phasing to
  achieve the successful integration of AIoT in smart tourism.

l Regulatory and Policy Frameworks: The regulatory environment on AIoT in
  tourism is also changing in many countries as guidelines on data protection, AI
  ethics, and adoption of technology are still being developed. Advances in AIoT
  technology leave governments and regulatory institutions with the challenge of
  developing policies that balance innovation with protection for the rights of the
  individual. Regulations are particularly important when dealing with data, and
  defining standards for personal information collection, storage, and sharing.
  Even a tourism operator dealing with tourists across jurisdictions may find this
  quite challenging to understand and adhere to. The system operators will have
  to pass through several local and international regulations in order to ensure
  proper compliance when crossing borders in a bid to avoid legal risks.
  Applying policy frameworks for innovation while prioritising public trust and
  safety is essential for the effective use of AIoT systems in tourism.
  Collaboration between governmental bodies, technology providers, and tour-
  ism industry operators can help create such frameworks, making it easier to
  deploy AIoT systems in compliance with legal and ethical standards.

Thus, some of the factors that may determine the uptake of AIoT in smart tourism
include careful thought about privacy and ethics, technical requirements, and reg-
ulatory requirements. Providing numerous benefits, AIoT is only viable if these
factors are addressed in the development of sustainable, trustworthy, and effective
AIoT-enabled systems for tourism. Notably, addressing such issues may be
accomplished by focusing on measures of transparency, inclusivity, and robust
solutions in using technological applications.

l Emerging Trends and Technologies: Among the many emerging trends and
  technologies that are expected to define the future of AIoT in smart transpor-
  tation for tourism, one of the most promising includes advancement in the area
  of autonomous vehicles. AVs promise to change the way people navigate
  destinations when fully deployed. As AV technology matures, we are going to
  see a significant shift in transportation dynamics with shuttles, buses, and taxis
  all acting as self-driving. For example, such vehicles would provide on-
  demand services. Travellers would, for the first time, be able to travel without
  necessarily needing to hire cars because of the provided service. In addition,
  5G networks will strongly and at a speedier rate connect AIoT devices, making
  transport systems more responsive and reliable. The real-time exchange of
  information will support the existence of complex AI algorithms that analyse
  traffic patterns, weather, and behaviour of visitors, allowing the optimisation
  of routes and schedules dynamically. Another trend includes the integration of
  advanced mobility solutions like mobility-as-a-service (MaaS) platforms. The
  MaaS platform will combine different modes of transport: public transit,
  ridesharing, bike-sharing, etc., into one intuitive app. It will allow tourists to
  gain hassle-free access to all modes of transport, thus enhancing their travel
  experiences.

l Opportunities for Future Human-AI Collaboration in Tourism: There is sig-
  nificant scope for even more advanced human-AI collaborations in tourism using
  AIoT systems. For instance, AI could help operators understand the way things are
  by resource allocation and customer services. For instance, with its analytical
  abilities, AI can forecast peak travel hours, while human operators can adjust
  services accordingly and provide the empathy and cultural understanding that
  technology cannot generate on its own. On the same note, AIoT systems allow
  tourists to have more personalised interactions based on their interests and beha-
  viours with other travellers whose previous behaviours and preferences shape
  recommendations tailored to their needs. For example, an AI would look at the
  travel history of a tourist and provide them with customised trip plans and trans-
  portation models, food among other things. This partnership would most likely
  lead to a richer experience for the human, further cementing ties between a tourist
  and his destination places. Workers in this industry will also have their workforce
  development done through AI as training programs and tools help to improve the
  skills of the workforce. For instance, AI simulation training will allow employees
  to simulate real-time response cases in customer service, crisis response cases, and
  operational procedures. This will lead to better overall service quality.

9.9 Conclusion

AIoT in smart transportation for tourism presents transformative opportunities by
enhancing the tourist experience, optimising operational efficiency, and promoting
sustainability in the industry. Key points highlight the potential uses of AIoT
technologies in delivering personalised tourism experiences, streamlining opera-
tions, and reducing environmental impacts. However, concerns remain regarding
privacy issues and the need for a strong infrastructure. Other key stakeholders
towards the successful implementation of AIoT in the tourism sector include
investing in infrastructures, focusing on data security, fostering cooperation,
encouraging innovation, and focusing towards the sustainability of practices.
Adapting human-centric and environmentally responsible approaches in the tour-
ism sector will rely more heavily on alignment with AIoT as the sector grows to
meet the evolving expectations of travellers and environmental challenges.
Embracing such trends will lead to a more intelligent, productive, and participatory
tourism industry that provides a value-adding experience for the traveller while
creating benefits for local communities and ecosystems.

10.1 Introduction

Sustainable mobility is the ability to meet the needs of transportation in the present
without impairing the ability of future generations to meet their own needs. It
emphasises efficient resource use, reduced environmental impact, and improved
quality of life [1]. As the pace of urbanisation and population growth continues to
grow, the need for transportation solutions that minimise negative impacts on the
environment becomes increasingly crucial. Among the most important sources of
greenhouse gas emissions, air pollution, and urban congestion are traditional
transport systems based on fossil fuels. Sustainable mobility encourages all public
transport alternatives in its use of bicycles, walking, electric, and even self-driving
vehicles, minimising the carbon footprint of transportation while enhancing
accessibility, equity, and safety for all users [2]. Moreover, sustainable mobility
includes smart technologies and data-driven approaches to optimise transportation
networks. Its development aims to improve traffic management, reduce travel
times, and enhance the overall efficiency of mobility solutions. AI and IoT in
autonomous vehicles are a transformation in the mobility landscape. Artificial
Intelligence of Things (AIoT) combines the analytical capabilities of AI with the
connectivity and data-sharing capabilities of IoT to enable real-time decisions in
vehicles, based on the vast data collected from sources such as sensors, cameras,
and infrastructure [3,4]. In the case of autonomous vehicles, AI algorithms process
data for navigation, detecting obstacles, and optimising driving behaviour. IoT
improves this process in the sense that communication among vehicles, infra-
structure, and other road users is enabled, thereby making the transportation eco-
system cohesive and efficient. This synergy helps in improved safety, less
congestion, and low emissions, in relation to sustainable mobility [5]. Predictive
maintenance and smart routing further advance efficiency in transportation systems
as regards AIoT technology. With the ability to predict possible problems and
optimise routes using real-time data, autonomous vehicles would work more effi-
ciently, thereby saving energy and energy-related environmental impacts. For an
array of reasons, it promotes sustainable mobility [6,7]. First, it deals with the
pressing need to fight climate change by reducing greenhouse gas emissions in the
transportation sector due to prevalent traditional modes of transport, which emit
huge amounts of greenhouse gases (GHG). New sustainable mobility solutions in
the form of electric and autonomous vehicles, powered by renewable sources of
energy, can significantly reduce the carbon footprint of the transport sector. It
improves public health because of the decreased air pollution and active modes of
transport, such as walking and cycling [8]. Good quality of air coupled with less
dependence on private vehicles helps in improving overall health results for urban
residents. Sustainable mobility fosters social equity since it enhances accessibility
for all people in a given community, regardless of economic status [9]. Public
transportation and shared mobility alternatives can ensure that transport-related
inequalities decline. Moreover, sustainable mobility adoption can catalyse eco-
nomic growth. This is because innovation in transport technologies will trigger the
development of new employment opportunities in areas like the manufacture of

electric vehicles, the development of smart infrastructure, and the transportation
service itself [10]. Cities and governments investing in sustainable mobility
initiatives can help foster resilience and adaptability in their transportation systems
to better match the needs of the residents with the environment [11]. More
importantly, the connection between sustainable mobility and AIoT in autonomous
vehicles paves the way for a brighter future by enhancing efficiency, promoting
equity, and protecting the environment within the transportation sector. Therefore,
by taking together the technologies and practices within a framework of integrative
development, we could approach the goals of sustainable mobility closer [12].
