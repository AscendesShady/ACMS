6.3.2 Incident detection and anomaly detection

Overall, the real-time detection of traffic incidents and anomalies is imperative to
ensure that disruptions are minimised and traffic flows safely. Events like a sudden
stop, an accident, or a road closure should be cleared as quickly as possible so that
traffic can keep flowing and the chances of an accident are minimised. Some
important AI techniques for detecting incidents are:

l Real-Time Anomaly Detection Methods: By finding ways in which traffic
  deviates from what is considered normal, AI models detect previously
  unknown malicious activity. Common techniques that are applied are statis-
  tical anomaly detection, rule-based models, and machine learning classifiers.
  These algorithms can be more complex deep learning approaches, such as the
  use of autoencoders that learn the normal traffic behaviour and identify
  divergences. So, if in a segment that normally flows freely, the flow suddenly
  slows and the density increases, the system recognises it as an anomaly, pos-
  sibly indicating an incident.

l Edge AI for Immediate Response: Edge AI is important for detecting incidents
  since they have lower latency in the process of anomaly detection. Rather than
  send data to be analysed in a central cloud, edge devices located close to the data
  source (for instance, at intersections or with traffic cameras) can do an initial
  anomaly detection locally. This method enhances response time, enabling the
  system to instantly identify an incident and notify relevant personnel or activate
  dynamic rerouting algorithms to direct traffic away from the incident location.

With real-time anomaly detection in place, AIoT systems can detect incidents and
respond much faster than what a human operator or a traditional control system
could do thus improving safety and ensuring efficiency in the traffic network.

6.4.1 Adaptive traffic signal control

AIoT Traffic Management: Adaptive traffic signal control is one of the most
effective AIoT applications to ensure efficient and smooth traffic management of

Reinforcement learning (RL) models can be trained to continuously optimise

l Reinforcement Learning Models for Signal Optimisation: Reinforcement
  learning for adaptive signal control, strength of reinforcement learning in

6.4.2 Route optimisation algorithms

Signal control is not the only aspect of good traffic management; real-time route
optimisation is also necessary to minimise travel time, alleviate congestion, and
keep drivers safe on the road. Algorithms that are leveraged for route optimisation
using AI will analyse real-time traffic conditions, predict the routes that will
eventually get most delayed, and provide on-demand routing solutions.

l Real-Time Dynamic Routing with AI-Based Optimisation: Most of the time,
  real-time routing decisions are taken based on Graph Algorithms such as A*,
  which is an efficient algorithm to find the shortest path in a weighted network.
  However, once the objective moves away from distance only (for example by
  avoiding congestion), mature AI methods like genetic algorithms (GAs) are used.
  Genetic algorithms are search metaheuristics made to imitate biological evolution,
  covering a broader scope of paths than others. For each vehicle, they evaluate
  these routes according to fitness criteria (such as travel time, congestion, safety
  metrics, etc.) and find an optimal/near-optimal route for every vehicle.
  AIoT for real-time traffic analysis and optimisation 109

l Dynamic Routing: Similarly, deep learning is used for dynamic routing,
  where AI models can learn in real-time from live traffic data to recommend
  nodes. This method allows agents to use feedback received on travel time and
  congestion to refine routing recommendations. An advantage of reinforcement
  learning-based routing is that it adapts in real-time, making sure that routes are
  constantly modified as soon as the traffic conditions change, thus reducing the
  chances of bottlenecks.

Traffic routing is multi-objective (and often conflicting), for example, the fastest path
may not be the most efficient or safe brewing path. This is where multi-objective
optimisation frameworks come in, as they optimise trade-offs between different
objectives, such as minimising travel time, fuel consumption, and risk of accidents.
Methods such as Pareto optimisation permit trade-offs between these objectives and
offer drivers or traffic management systems routes that are optimal based on prevailing
priorities. For instance, during high pollution periods, routes will be optimised to
produce less emissions; but in case of emergency, it will prefer the fastest routes.

  The real-time AI-based adaptive routing algorithms improve urban mobility by
providing contextual and demand-oriented guidance for drivers and cars to elim-
inate jams and enhance safety.

6.4.3 Load balancing in traffic networks

Another very important optimisation technique is load balancing because asym-
metry in the huge volume of traffic flow might congest certain regions of the road
network, while other regions have low-level traffic flow; thus, the efficiency of the
overall traffic is poor. In many urban networks, especially complex modes of
transportation [14], traditional techniques are rarely capable of providing predictive
ability in demand management. Load balancing with the help of AIoT also provides
predictive and adaptive power to traffic distribution, enabling a more proactive way
of managing traffic.

l Managing Traffic Distribution to Avoid Congestion Hotspots: Load bal-
  ancing emphasises preventing vehicle congestion by redistributing the vehicles
  to circumvent possible bottlenecks. Analysing real-time data as well as his-
  torical traffic patterns, AI algorithms identify potential congestion points and
  dynamically adjust the flow of traffic to relieve congestion pressure on the
  busiest route. Vehicles, for example, can be covered up during rush hours from
  parts of the city where there are regular enhancements and rather routed
  through alternative pathways.

l AI Techniques for Predictive Load Balancing: AI models, specifically pre-
  dictive models, are used to control how traffic load is distributed in networks.
  Machine learning models can use historical congestion data and real-time
  traffic flows to identify areas, in advance, that are likely to see high demand
  and proactively rerouting strategies to minimise the impact of congestion. In
  this context, we apply support vector machines (SVMs) and deep neural net-
  work (DNNs) techniques for forecasting time and location-specific traffic
  density [15].
110 AIoT for smart transportation

  Load balancing is also one of the main applications of reinforcement
  learning between multi-agent systems since every traffic management point
  (like a signal or roundabout) can be seen as an agent itself. The problem is then
  solved by these agents working together to optimise the available traffic load
  in the network concerning historical, current, and future traffic flows. For
  example, if the traffic in one area is increasing, nearby agents can send vehi-
  cles to less congested routes and distribute the load evenly on the network.
  Taking a predictive and distributed approach where congestion hotspots are
  avoided from developing, not only makes the network more resilient by
  reducing congestion events but ensures a higher network level of efficiency.

  only does this ability contribute to the short-term mitigation of traffic impacts,

  directly identify atypical movement signals suggestive of an accident or bot-
  tlenecks and can autonomously change time-sensitive traffic signals or inform
  drivers, enabling real-time onsite modifications that classic systems simply
  cannot produce.

6.7 Conclusion

AIoT is a new paradigm of technologies to be combined, which creates an unpre-
cedented opportunity to rethink the urban traffic system. IoT and AI working
together provide the power to make informed data-based decisions on traffic
management allowing cities to move from a reactive approach to a proactive one.
Roadways are made intelligent (improving traffic flows, reducing congestion, and
enhancing road safety) using AIoT with adaptive signal control, predictive mod-
elling, and dynamic routing. An AIoT system optimises traffic flows across the
landscape. Implementation of 5G and edge computing also adds to the speed,
responsiveness, and agility in decision-making from systems. Digital twins and
federated AI can continue to drive innovations in traffic optimisation as new
approaches are explored. A scalable and resilient deployment of AIoT systems will
become increasingly needed in the age of urbanisation to serve the ever-changing
needs of smart cities. This research provides a strong foundation for AIoT-based
traffic solutions, contributing to intelligent and sustainable urban mobility.

7.2 Data sources and IoT sensors in smart transportation

7.2.1 Data sources

7.2.1.1 Vehicle-based data sources
Most of the vehicle-centric data in relation to vehicles and their operations as well
as their interactions within their immediate environment are real time in nature and
122 AIoT for smart transportation

are primarily obtained from vehicle-oriented sources. On-board diagnostics
(OBDs) of vehicles contain information about the performance of the featured car,
its fuel efficiency, and diagnostic codes for various problems. Telematics devices
make fleet management and logistics solutions possible since they allow tracking of
vehicles and their control via GPS and cell phone communication technology.

  Current and Doppler devices, an odometer, internal and external antennas, and
microphones are examples of in-car additional sensors that provide much more
information about the location, movement, and surroundings of a car. Such infor-
mation can be utilised in a large number of applications, including real-time traffic
tracking, self-driving car routing, or coordinate systems for assisted driving aids
[8]. V2X is also known as connected vehicle systems which facilitate data
exchange between vehicles, infrastructure, pedestrians, and others a V2V, V2I, and
V2P, respectively. Increased traffic safety, management of traffic flow, and
operations of the independent vehicle systems are other advantages of data sharing.

7.2.1.2 Infrastructure-based data sources
It is also noteworthy that information produced by transportation infrastructure is
crucial for ITS functioning and decision-making. Some of the traffic monitoring
instruments that acquire information including incidence identification and traffic
fluctuations and densities include inductive loops, radar, and video cameras.
Various factors that influence transportation conditions such as road surface,
temperature, precipitation, and other related phenomena are detected by road
weather information systems (RWIS) [9]. The traffic control and management
create data through the management systems in ITS including ramp metering
systems and traffic signals as well as variable message signs. With this data,
systems particularly for traveller information can be improved and traffic con-
gestion as well as its bottlenecks can also be reduced. Based on RFID and auto-
matic number-plate recognition (ANPR) innovation, toll collection systems
generate data on vehicle traffic and travel patterns that are beneficial for transport
planning and modelling.

7.2.1.3 Mobile data sources
Thanks to the use of smartphones and, especially, mobile applications, there are
more opportunities to use crowdsourcing for data collecting in the transport sector
nowadays. Social and regional services for portable devices, for example, Waze
and Google Navigational Map, monitor GPS and other client information to
transfer current traffic conditions, mishaps, and bearings. These platforms offer
crowdsourced traffic data which can be integrated with other traditional data to
enhance understanding of current traffic conditions and for dynamic real-time
traffic routing and optimisation.

7.2.1.4 External data sources
While the primary data are collected from the transport ecosystem, other sources
are valuable for enhancing the efficiency of highly over-sophisticated transport
systems. The decisions and functions that concern transportation, may be

LiDAR or various high-quality camera sensors are applied in self-driving cars since
they provide an expansively broad picture of the surroundings of a car. There are
several categories of data, and deep learning algorithms to identify the objects and
classify them and then offer a safe and optimal navigation path.

7.2.2.4 Location and positioning sensors
Many transportation solutions require location and positioning information
including fleet management, asset tracking, and navigation. For automobiles or
infrastructure parts or for highly portable smart mobiles, the GPS receivers in
combination with positioning solutions like GLONASS or Galileo, provide accu-
rate location information. Others include INS and RTK systems are used in trans-
port operations like navigation of self-driving cars and surveying of transport
infrastructure for which precise details of location of positioning are necessary but
not directly derived from GPS.

7.2.2.5 Motion and occupancy sensors
In transportation infrastructures or related vehicles, motion and occupancy sensors
are used to detect any vibrations, motion, or occupancy respectively. These sensors
can be used in a lot of areas, for example, structural health monitoring, counting the
number of passengers, and systems for diagnostic of cars. The vibrations and
rotations of a vehicle are expressed by way of gyroscopes and accelerometers; such
measurements may be quite useful in collision avoidance systems, monitoring
driver behaviour, as well as in interesting stability systems. For better resource
management and service enhancement, the number of customers entering or exiting
public transportation stations is counted using occupancy sensors including infrared
or ultrasonic sensors.

7.2.2.6 Pressure and weight sensors
Public transportation and weather are used to monitor traffic and road conditions
and to evaluate vehicle performance in ITS. These sensors assist with structural
integrity assessment and vehicle safety, for example, by measuring tyre pressure as
well as vehicle load and weight distribution. By detecting low pressure on the tyres,
tire pressure monitoring system (TPMS) enhances the benefits of fuel conservation
and avoidance of accidents caused by tyre blowouts. By detecting the weight of
travelling vehicles, weigh-in-motion (WIM) sensors which are fitted on roads offer
data for tolling, pavement maintenance, or road design. In addition, weight sensors
are applied in warehouses and transportation to ensure the right distribution of
loads and compliance with the weight norms in the sphere of material flow, which
in turn helps to optimise processes and enhance safety.

7.2.2.7 Sound and vibration sensors
Auditory information and vibration patterns are measured and detected by vibration
as well as sound sensors through ITS and these sensors help in monitoring noise
levels, diagnosing vehicles and as well as in assessing the condition of the infra-
structure. The assistance of microphones and acoustic sensors can also allow
authorities to make sure that throughout transportation corridors, environmental
norms are observed, as well as noise decrease means are exercised. Accelerometers
and piezoelectric sensors are examples of vibration sensors that are used in the
assessment of the status of such components as railroads and bridges. They can help
to indicate which future breakdowns or structural abnormalities may occur by
measuring abnormal vibrations and hence help to prevent them. Engineers have
used sound and vibration sensors in diagnosing automobile engines. These sensors
detect things such as inaccuracies or even bearing damages and through this,
maintenance can be done before it is due hence enhancing vehicle dependability.

widespread use of IoT-based sensors in transportation applications – such as asset

ing apart from the management of the assets.

7.3 Data acquisition and communication

7.3.1 Data acquisition techniques

7.3.1.1 Wired data acquisition
Copper-based solutions like Ethernet and serial communication interfaces still
remain a preferred tool for data acquisition from fixed structures namely the
ITS systems. There exists extensive utilisation of Ethernet-based networks for
transferring data from traffic monitoring stations, toll plazas, and other fixed
instrumentation to the decentralised control centres. Some of such protocols
include RS-232 and RS-485 which are mainly used in low-speed data acquisition
from incompatible systems and other devices for short distance long haul com-
munication at affordable expense.

7.3.1.2 Wireless data acquisition
The use of wireless technology: Due to the increased coverage areas of smart
transportation systems, wireless communication technologies play a central role in
accumulating data from moving sources and distant areas. Mobile networks, such
as 4G/LTE and the new, evolving 5G, guarantee pervasive access to the network
for delivering data from cars, portable devices, and IoT sensors placed in the
environment. Most applications for short-range wireless data acquisition can
employ Wi-Fi and Bluetooth, which enables the communication between vehicles,
roadside units, and personal devices operating in a given geographic region.
Commit short-range communications (CSRC) is a focused short-range wireless
protocol tailored for V2V and V2I applications for safety-relevant message
exchanging and real-time data transmission for ITS applications [12].
