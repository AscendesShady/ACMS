Abstract

In the modern world, the application of artificial intelligence to intelligent transpor-
tation systems is a developing field. Ensuring efficiency and addressing safety and
security concerns have already become essential measures. Predictive maintenance
(PdM) is made possible by real-time data analysis, suggesting that infrastructure and
machinery may be able to detect early warning signs of an impending failure.
Moreover, AI-powered monitoring systems continuously analyse data from sensors
deployed across transportation networks, allowing for proactive identification of
anomalies and prompt response to safety threats. This means that threats are detected
and eliminated without users’ interference. It also optimises traffic control, leading to
considerable declines in both jams and accidents. These issues are managed by
adaptive technology that adjusts the flow of tasks based on real-time information.
AIoT is heavily utilised in driver assistance systems that provide warnings and
impose assistance immediately to reduce the likelihood of accidents. The AIoT cal-
culates the best way to respond to an accident and use the available resources more
effectively. This chapter aims to emphasise the revolutionary impact of AIoT tech-
nology on improving the safety and security of intelligent transportation systems,
which consequently results in more reliable, effective, and sustainable means of
mobility for communities across the globe. The purpose of the chapter is to provide
an in-depth analysis of the methods in which AIoT techniques can be employed for
various types of transportation applications, including, but not limited to, emergency
response, traffic control, driver assistance, and PdM. In addition, the chapter will
address the benefits and challenges of using AIoT in the transportation industry
through a range of case studies and real-life scenarios.

8.1 Overview of AIoT in smart transportation

The rapid urbanisation of cities in developing nations like India has caused a
sharp rise in the number of cars on the road in recent years. This heavily puts
pressure on the transport system and creates an alarming rate of negative
environmental impacts like heavy traffic jams, air pollution, frequent traffic
accidents, etc. Conventional traffic management methods have numerous issues
as a result of inadequate communication between vehicles and vehicle-to-
infrastructure [1]. The world will thus see an enormous expansion in motor cars
due to the increase in the number of people living in metropolitan areas. This will
have a negative impact and contribute to issues such as road accidents, noise
pollution, traffic congestion, and other problems. Because of the increased reli-
ance on transportation systems in contemporary society, a person often has to
cope with a wide range of problems related to these systems on a daily basis.
These problems can include traffic jams, parking issues with a shortage of
spaces, longer commutes, high CO2 emissions, an increase in accidents, and
many more. It is essential to use information technology more frequently to
increase the operational efficacy of transportation systems [10]. The definition of
Intelligent Transportation Systems also known as Smart Transportation, is ‘the
application of advanced sensor, computer, electronics, and communication
technologies, as well as management strategies in an integrated manner to
improve the surface transportation system’s safety and efficiency’ [11]. In
addition to cutting travel times and fuel consumption, smart transportation
solutions increase traffic flow and safety. The development of Intelligent Traffic
Systems to enhance traffic flow and safety has been spurred by the progress
made in wireless communication and Internet capabilities. One of the main
elements influencing Intelligent Transport System (ITS) effectiveness is the
most recent advancements in communication technology. Effective commu-
nication may greatly increase safety while also assisting in lowering traffic and
gas emissions to an absolute minimum. Figure 8.1 shows the various applications
of smart transport systems.

  However, in recent years, researchers have been inspired to reconsider and
redevelop ITS by the Internet of Things (IoT) and the idea of Vehicular Ad-hoc
Networks (VANETs) [2]. The employment of electronics in automobiles has
enabled certain tasks that were previously thought to be mechanically impractical.
Examples include location monitoring devices in cars, accident alert systems,
electronic toll collection systems, and artificial intelligence. Despite the sharing of
many parts, these systems are marketed as separate systems with distinct protocols.
The most powerful modern mobility solution is smart transportation systems that
use Artificial Intelligence of Things (AIoT). The IoT and artificial intelligence are
integrated for real data analysis. AIoT facilitates seamless communication between
people, infrastructure, and vehicles in all aspects of transportation, creating an
ecosystem that is more responsive, intelligent, and effective. The components of

8.1.1 Importance of safety and security in transportation
  systems

When it comes to the health and well-being of people, safety and security are the top-most
priorities for transportation. Transporters face many problems other than traffic accidents,
crashes, and breakdowns. By implementing AIoT technology, one can take proactive
security measures and anticipate the problems before they escalate [3]. It is essential to
cultivate public trust and support for intelligent transportation, as a strong security system
is required. The safety and security in any transportation are of utmost importance as they
have a bearing on national security, economic prosperity, and public well-being.

8.1.1.1 Public health
Transportation networks are primarily designed to move people and things across
space. The public’s faith and assurance in these systems will only be maintained
when passenger and cargo safety is maintained. In case of mishaps and security
lapses, there can be loss of lives, injuries, and psychological repercussions for both
the survivors and society at large. By performing routine maintenance, educating
drivers on safety, and using advanced safety systems, we can lower the risk of
accidents. Security measures such as monitoring, controlling access, and detection
of threats help us a lot to avoid vandalism and terrorism.

8.1.1.2 A stable economy
The networks of transportation systems are facilitators of global trade and business.
Events derailed by security threats or safety breaches can lead the economy astray.
156 AIoT for smart transportation

Whether a security breach at a major port or thousands of miles from home, a col-
lision on the interstate interrupts supply chains increasing costs and delaying ship-
ments [4]. By investing in robust safety and security capabilities, the movement of
goods and services is facilitated, providing resilience against potential interruptions
caused by tailbacks. This in turn incentivises the creation of reliable, efficient
transportation networks that further support economic stability and growth.

8.1.1.3 Defence of deregulation
Transportation Infrastructure is a key part of National Security. Secure transport
would protect from questionable actions, terrorism, and smuggling. The main-
tenance and security of transportation networks require cooperation between gov-
ernmental organisations, commercial enterprises, and international partners [5].
Together, these steps will provide a global approach to security planning, help
identify potential vulnerabilities, and enhance the overall resilience of various
transportation networks against attacks.

8.1.1.4 Advances in technology
There are so many new technological innovations that could be implemented which
would result in enhanced security and safety for transportation networks including
the use of more sophisticated AI. Artificial intelligence (AI), powerful surveillance
systems, and driverless cars could lead to a total removal of transportation security.
Similarly, autonomous cars would eliminate human error which is responsible for
the majority of the accidents. AI can detect potential security threats by analysing a
large amount of data [6]. This means solving other problems like cyber security
threats that stem as a result of adopting many different technologies to ensure they
are safely and securely integrated.

8.1.1.5 The information you need
Strong laws and rules are essential for maintaining and improving the security and
safety of transportation networks. Governments are necessary to create laws, enforce
them, and provide oversight. Policies must have the agility to address emerging risks
and technologies. The legislative frameworks should ‘find the right balance’ in making
room for innovation that does not compromise security and safety. As transportation
networks often cross national borders, global security problems will be solved by
combining the efforts and unifying the legislation at an international level [7].

8.1.1.6 Education and public awareness
Another primary ingredient in any comprehensive safety and security plan is edu-
cation and public awareness. The security and safety of the transportation system
can be ensured by promoting awareness campaigns in trains through rail rider
programs illustrating the first response to emergency preparedness and commer-
cials/ awareness surrounding public transit on security protocols. Campaigns, talks,
and occurrences that exhibit a good community attitude create an environment
where people feel secure – it not only enables them to be vigilant but also helps
them respond in smarter ways when facing various circumstances and obstacles.

8.2 The role of AIoT in predictive maintenance

With the arrival of predictive maintenance (PdM), timed-based schedules are now a
thing of the past for industrial maintenance. A vital contribution to this revolution
has come from The AIoT – an intersection between the IoT and AI. AIoT averts
machinery failures by anticipating preferred hardware maltreatment hence,
enhancing the maintenance operational steps and reducing process expenses [8]. It
accomplishes this by combining the connectivity and data collection functions of
IoT devices with AI’s advanced data analysis and predictive machine learning
(ML) algorithms, as illustrated in Figure 8.2.

  Collection and Aggregation of Data: AIoT powers PdM as it allows self-
monitoring of installed sensors on industrial setups to collect continuous data.
These sensors measure multiple parameters including temperature, vibration,
pressure, humidity, and many more; the data obtained is accumulated in real time
and released to centralised platforms for further analysis. Smooth integration of
many data streams through IoT devices provides comprehensive, real-time insight
into the operational state of the equipment.

  A large part of the foundation of inflammation testing comes from techniques
in machine learning and data analysis where artificial intelligence systems piece
through heaps of information after it has been collected, looking for patterns that
would indicate a future issue or an aberrancy. Machine learning is used to train the
model with historical data, so that performance degradation or wear and tear, etc.
can be detected beforehand. These models improve accuracy over time as they
continuously learn from new data and give us more confidence in the forecasts [9].

8.7 Emergency response optimisation

Bringing AIoT into emergency response optimisation is a remarkable step forward
to make the entire service more effective and efficient. By integrating Artificial

Intelligence (AI) with the IoT, emergency response systems can enhance the

transacted from a variety of sources such as sensors, wearable technology, and
mobile applications. Cloud-based AI algorithms analyse incoming data to predict
where disasters will strike next, identify the most vulnerable regions, and optimise
response times. For example, AIoT can prioritise ambulance dispatch based on the
availability of hospital resources, the conditions of roads travelled to get there, and

which will improve the effective operations of emergency response [17].

spread patterns as well and enabled preventive action to minimise the effect on

8.8 Autonomous vehicles and AIoT

From early Driver Support Systems (DSSs) to fully autonomous cars that are
capable of driving through complex urban environments, the technologies for
autonomous vehicles (AVs) have improved. The initial advances focused on cam-
eras and sensors to allow rudimentary object identification and collision avoidance –
things such as radar and LiDAR. Later, it was incorporated with sophisticated AI
algorithms over time, thus enabling real-time data processing into corresponding
decision-making [10]. IoT: an essential part of security in a driverless car that utilises
machine learning algorithms that are constantly connecting information from the
vehicle with their global AI algorithms, autonomous cars (AV) can essentially teach
them how to adapt to new driving situations. The driving tactics rely on such input
data to ensure that the vehicle can navigate safely, even in unforeseen situations, as
these may vary from one street to another and depend on factors such as traffic lights,
slippery roads, or similar conditions. Besides serving as a liaison between vehicles
and infrastructure, AIoT systems actively contribute to safer traffic by coordinating
movements and preventing collisions.

  One of the biggest advantages that cars with AI have is their potential to totally
eradicate human error, which is a huge cause of traffic accidents. AIoT systems can
consistently make decisions in real-time with a high level of accuracy that would
likely not be possible for humans as well. This reduces the chance of accidents
resulting from human factors such as fatigue, inattentiveness, or error on a person’s
part. It provides extensive and present data that allows AIoT to make decisions for
autonomous cars [9]. Machine learning algorithms analyse patterns to predict
potential threats, allowing reactive responses to become proactive. An autonomous
car could predict, for example as a result of historical data and instant observation
when a possible pedestrian may want to cross the road and adjust its speed and
trajectory thus-level.

8.9 Challenges and considerations

Despite these advancements, the incorporation of AIoT into smart transportation
systems faces several operational and technical challenges. Understanding the
  AIoT-based safety and security in smart transportation 169

complex AI algorithms and managing these algorithms is still a challenging task.
Ensuring data integrity and allowing for reliable and instantaneous device-to-
device communication is also a key task while retaining performance. The com-
patibility of various systems and standards additionally remains a key challenge.

  Ethical and Privacy Issues: Using AIoT in transportation also opens up ethical
and privacy concerns. Ongoing data collection and monitoring can lead to possible
privacy violations. There is also the matter of morality – when it all comes down to
hard decisions. As an example, a driverless car may need to determine the relative
value of different sets of human lives when allocating road space between pedes-
trians and (possibly paying) passengers [8].

  Regulatory and Compliance: The new regulatory frameworks (especially
around autonomous vehicles) are evolving. Standards for interoperability, safety,
and data security are needed to ensure the wide application of these technologies.
Maintaining compliance with existing transportation laws, such as crafting new
regulations around AIoT-specific challenges, is an ongoing process.

  Future Trends and Opportunities of AIoT in Transportation: The prevailing
blossoming trend with intelligent system integration, leveraging transportation with
AIOT seems to be quite a promising opportunity for the automotive field. With
technologies like Artificial Intelligence, Machine Learning, and 5G taking shape,
more advanced apps can be developed. For everything from personalised mobility
services to smart city infrastructure and eco-friendly transportation alternatives, the
opportunities are there.

8.10 Conclusion

One of the most crucial ways of upgrading security and safety is by imple-
menting AIoT in intelligent transportation networks. AIoT shortens emergency
response times and enables PdM practices and traffic prediction based on real-
time data with deep analytics. The AIoT-based autonomous vehicles outperform
in terms of the decision-making process and this leads to less number of acci-
dents. The AIoT-based transportations are more efficient, secure, robust as well
as sustainable.

  AIoT advancements will allow for innovation in mobility modalities such as
autonomous public transport and on-demand car hire. These suggestions will get
cars off the road, diminish pollution and enable transport for everybody. As AIoT
grows, it will play a greater role in securing smart transportation systems.
Stakeholders must work together and invest in AIoT and do thorough research to
realise its potential for safer, more efficient transportation across communities
globally.
