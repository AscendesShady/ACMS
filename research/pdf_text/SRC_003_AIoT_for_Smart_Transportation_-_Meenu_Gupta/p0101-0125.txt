Abstract

Today the major empowering technology in the advancement of Industry 4.0 is
Artificial Intelligence (AI) and the Internet of Things (IoT). The combination of AI
techniques with the IoT has given rise to the field of AIoT. This improved the
quality of services and the level of intelligence. Mobility 5.0 offers sustainability,
efficiency, and accessibility through smart transportation systems. Currently, smart
transportation systems undergo issues like security, integrity, unauthorised access,
and other performance-related problems. To achieve robust methodologies that
address these issues and enhance complex functionalities, integration of AI algo-
rithms and IoT is essential. To offer innovative services in smart transportation like
Intelligent Transportation Systems (ITS), intelligent traffic and logistics manage-
ment, supply chain management, and many others, the capabilities of AIoT will
help to design robust, sustainable, and efficient frameworks. This chapter reviews
several aspects and applications of the intelligent transport system based on AIoT.
The challenges and research opportunities to provide and implement ITS for opti-
mising traffic patterns, reducing congestion, and improving efficiency are also
discussed.

  Keywords: Industry 5.0; Internet of Things; Artificial Intelligence of Things;
  Intelligent Transportation Systems; Machine Learning; Cyber Security;
  Society 5.0.

5.1 Introduction

For many years, the transportation sector has undergone evolution throughout the
years. Every industrial revolution introduced innovations that revolutionised
transportation, resulting in increased speed, higher safety, and greater efficiency.

Although AI and IoT were essential in Industry 4.0 for facilitating automation and

edge technologies to transform the transportation sector. The objective of incor-
porating intelligent technologies into transportation networks is to improve effi-
ciency, safety, and sustainability. Industry 4.0, emphasises data interchange and
automation, whereas Industry 5.0 combines artificial intelligence (AI), human
intelligence, and Internet of Things (IoT), constituting AIoT. Artificial Intelli-

and data-gathering mechanisms of IoT. This integration helps systems to inde-

more intelligent and effective operations. Intelligent transportation plays a crucial

include [2],

l Use of smart sensors: Smart sensors are used for obtaining real-time updates

l Communication networks: Vehicular traffic management is handled using
  vehicle-to-vehicle (V2V) and vehicle-to-infrastructure (V2I) protocols

l Safety and security: Addresses security challenges like unauthorised access
  and data sharing and improves travel safety

5.2 Role of IoT and ML in smart transportation

greatly revolutionised the field of ITS. The current research work focuses on
improving efficiency, safety, and sustainability in transportation. Information and

networks and quick data interchange and administration. It supports the optimisa-

With the help of IoT, condition monitoring can be achieved to improve safety. It
addresses issues that occur due to human errors and helps to minimise transporta-

vehicles but also in railway transportation systems, the use of IoT and ML can offer

maintenance. Telematics devices in vehicles provide updates related to fuel usage,

5.3.1 Ethical considerations of autonomous decision-making
  in Connected and Autonomous Vehicles (CAVs)
  in transport systems

l Accountability and Transparency – Ethical transparency involves clearly
  understanding how CAVs make decisions, especially in critical situations.
  Accountability concerns arise when an autonomous vehicle is part of an acci-
  dent; determining who is responsible; the vehicle’s manufacturer, the software
  developers, or the vehicle owner becomes crucial. Clear guidelines and
  accountability mechanisms are necessary to address these issues [22,23].

l The Trolley Problem and Moral Algorithms – A major ethical issue in
  autonomous vehicles is akin to the ‘trolley problem’, a philosophical dilemma
  about making choices that involve harm. In scenarios where a vehicle must
  choose between actions that lead to different outcomes, such as choosing
  whom to harm or protect, the programming of these decisions reflects moral
  judgements. For instance, if an unavoidable collision occurs, should the vehi-
  cle prioritise the safety of its occupants over pedestrians, or vice versa?
  [22,24].

l Fairness and Bias – Bias in the algorithms of autonomous vehicles can lead to
  unfair treatment and decisions. If algorithms are trained on biased data, they
  may produce discriminatory outcomes. Addressing and mitigating such biases
  is essential to ensure fairness and avoid perpetuating existing inequalities
  [25,26].

l Privacy Concerns – CAVs generate substantial amounts of data about their
  surroundings, passengers, and operational status. Confirming data privacy is a
  critical ethical issue to avoid unauthorised access or misuse [27].

l Standardised communication protocols
l Unified data formats
l Interoperability frameworks and middleware
l Semantic interoperability

To reduce fragmentation and promote smooth integration across the devices, open
standards need to be adopted. The open standards facilitate compatibility and
support innovation. Standardised communication protocols are essential for this
purpose. Constrained Application Protocol (CoAP) and Message Queuing
Telemetry Transport (MQTT) offer a common framework for communication of
devices and systems [21,28]. Utilising unified data formats facilitates consistency
and compatibility across different systems. Common formats such as JSON, XML,
and Protocol Buffers ensure that the data can be easily shared and understood
between various platforms [20], whereas, the middleware solutions and interoper-
ability frameworks provide a layer of abstraction that helps manage communica-
tion, data translation, and integration between different systems. These tools are
crucial for bridging the gaps between various technologies [29,30]. Semantic
interoperability involves ensuring that the meaning of data remains consistent
across different systems. Utilising ontologies and semantic frameworks like Web
Ontology Language (OWL) as well as Resource Description Framework (RDF)
enhances the ability of systems to interpret and use shared data effectively [20].

  Developments of the interoperability test beds allow the testing and validation
of the system compatibility beforehand to help identify integration issues and
ensure that systems work together as required. Collaboration between stakeholders
such as device manufacturers, standards organisations, and platform providers is
essential for achieving interoperability. Joint efforts in developing and promoting
common standards can facilitate better integration and functionality.

l Edge Processing Layer: This layer performs the function of computation
  closer to the data sources using edge devices like the intelligent gateway, and
  local servers for the initial preprocessing and analysis of data. Edge processing
  also helps reduce network load by filtering, aggregating, and providing initial
  analytics operations on data before transmission to central processing units,
  and not just latency reduction.
