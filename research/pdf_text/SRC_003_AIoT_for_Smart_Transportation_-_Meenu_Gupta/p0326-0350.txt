  Additionally, providing precise position data and traffic intelligence to emer-
gency response teams facilitates their route planning. Artificial intelligence (AI)
has enabled these advancements in coordination, which might save lives. Instead of
taking the “now or never” stance that has characterized some of humanity’s
attempts to reduce the number of deaths associated with automobile travel, some AI
work enables a much more nuanced understanding of when, when, and how these
high-risk circumstances develop.

  Moreover, when AI is merged with vehicle-to-infrastructure communication,
drivers are offered up-to-the-moment alerts about forthcoming perils or changes to
the traffic pattern. The end result of these developments is a safer and more
dependable transportation environment for all roadway users.

14.9 Public transportation optimization

AI enhances the operation of public transport systems. This stems from their cap-
ability to simplify complex tasks, in addition to the close composite relationship
between public transport patronage and road traffic congestion. Most major public
transport systems occupy the same rights-of-way as road traffic. When public
transport vehicles are delayed or otherwise inefficient, unjustified road space is
occupied. The converse is also true: When road traffic is congested, unjustified
road space is occupied. AI, therefore, has a strong potential payoff in the public
transport sector.

14.9.1 Demand prediction and scheduling

Public transit is one area where AI can have a huge positive influence on systems
and the services they deliver. This is because they can be optimized with algorithms
that infallibly predict the demand for transportation. These algorithms draw from a
rich pool of data, use historical patterns, and, most importantly, leverage real-time
information about all sorts of conditions. By using these techniques, they may make
sure that during periods of high demand, buses, trains, and other public transpor-
tation vehicles operate more often, and during periods of low demand, they run less
frequently.

  Moreover, AI can take vehicle upkeep to a new level. Foreseeing the kind of
wear and tear that might lead to malfunctioning components and erratic system
behaviours can help ensure that the fleet remains operational and that all vehicles
keep up with their scheduled maintenance. This kind of reliability is crucial for the
agencies that run fleets, not only to keep up with the demand but also to ensure that
all the vehicles in the fleet are functioning and that all passengers are safe.

  In a bid to up the ante on convenience, the real-time alert feature now available
through the mobile app allows for instant communication about any transportation
changes. If a train or bus is running behind schedule, the information will flow directly
to the customer. If a route has been altered, that news will be conveyed, too. The
improvements, in total, push for a public transit system that is as highly accom-
modating as possible to the not-nearly-as-regularly-scheduled lives of its riders.

14.9.2 Route optimization and efficiency

Route optimization for public transit is a significant challenge. Different from the case
of private vehicles, where drivers exercise a certain degree of freedom in choosing their
routes, transit agencies can have limited success in regulating the paths their vehicles
take. AI-powered route optimization attempts to overcome the great amounts of traffic.
The main factors and limitations that these systems are made to manage include traffic,
road construction, and events that may arise along the route. Of course, one other
variable is time: transit passengers have to arrive at their destination close to the time
they would likely have arrived had they driven their own vehicle.

14.10 Adaptive traffic control systems

Adaptive traffic control systems based on AI are constantly learning. They learn
from real-time traffic data and from how traffic behaves over time. We give them
the information they need to learn – like what happened to traffic when signal
timings were set one way versus another. We also tell them what “delay” means
and how it should be reduced. Then they go to work, reducing delays and
improving the overall efficiency of traffic flow.

14.10.1 Real-time traffic signal adjustment

Adaptive traffic control systems powered by AI are now in vogue. They utilize
real-time data gathered from various traffic monitoring devices. These devices
include conventional traffic sensors and cameras mounted around intersections and
connected vehicles. The modern traffic engineer’s data collection tool would now
appear rather close to the eye of a vintage neighbourhood private investigator. AI-
based adaptive traffic control systems use this data to “see” when an intersection is
malfunctioning. Based on the intersection’s momentary volume, congestion, and
flow condition, AI modifies refined “good enough” timing adjustments for the
signals controlling traffic into, through, and out of the intersection. By using these
devices to gather contemporary data, the engineer can make use of something akin
to the traffic patterns gathered by the average motorist’s memory.

14.10.2 Reduced congestion and improved fuel efficiency

Intelligent adaptive traffic controls can ease traffic congestion. They do this by
altering the flow of vehicles in real-time at busy intersections and along urban
roadways. In doing so, they head off bottlenecks and shorten both the frequency
and the length of traffic jams. These gadgets are the cornerstone of a more envir-
onmentally friendly and sustainable transportation system when we take into
account their impact on fuel efficiency, vehicle emissions, and trip effectiveness.
Moreover, adaptive traffic management systems increase road safety by guaran-
teeing improved traffic flow and lowering the likelihood of collisions brought on by
unexpected halts or traffic jams. These technologies lower road maintenance costs
and increase the life span of road infrastructure since they help optimize traffic
itself: the number of vehicles successfully reaching their destinations will always

be less than the number that would reach their destination if traffic were jammed.
“Jam” is an unsustainable state, and AI can more effectively resolve this problem.

14.11 Challenges and considerations

The most recent studies have pointed out the possible benefits and drawbacks of
combining AI with transport systems. According to Nikitas et al. [26], the risk of
certain software and hardware failures – a risk whose main competitor in the
engineering trade-off of risks and rewards is improved efficiency and better safety.
Meanwhile, privacy remains a hard nut for AI in transport to crack because the data
relations between individuals, machines, and all those corresponding databases are
bound to be really intricate, and any breach in that relationship can shortcut its way
to unavoidable privacy violations that all AI systems must contend with. Finally,
one must not overlook the risk of integrating AI in transport, leading to an uptick in
accidents during the early years of the transition to a fully AI-driven system.

  These difficulties have not made it any less urgent to ensure the privacy and
security of people’s data. People collect data from very big and important sources.
A multitude of sensors, like traffic cameras and GPS units, provide data streams
that are both essential to operations and alluring to would-be miscreants. The
integrity and trustworthiness of the transportation network depend on implementing
cutting-edge cybersecurity measures.

  Ethics also has a vital role. For instance, if an AI-based algorithm is biased, it
could unfairly impact a group of people that the system is serving. Take an AI-run
ridesharing service, for instance. If the system uses biased historical data, it could
unfairly affect people living in certain neighbourhoods by granting them fewer
rides that are more accessible to other (non-biased) neighbourhoods. Whether one
agrees or not, fairness is an ethical consideration that has to be taken into account.

  One must also take on the challenging problems of governance and law. The
accidents that do occur have to be compensated in a legally responsible manner.
Everyone involved in the AV ecosystem has to be fully aware of their legal
responsibilities if one is to have trust in them. This suggests that maintaining road
safety is necessary even in the absence of human drivers. For these road-holding
humans, the Federal Automated Vehicles Policy, issued by the National Highway
Traffic Safety Administration [1], is nothing short of a way to ensure “regulatory
calm,” making sure that AVs do not violate human rights in any way – the right to
life and the right to privacy. This, in turn, means ensuring that road-holding humans
are safe from AVs and that AVs are safe from road-holding humans.

  At present, the AV START Act is a proposed law, but if it is enacted, it will set
forth a national framework for the rollout of autonomous vehicles, moving them
along towards safe, nationwide deployment. In the European Union, on the other
hand, the national framework is actually a series of directives, such as the General
Data Protection Regulation [7], that concerns privacy issues related to the vast
amounts of data collected by vehicles. The Commission of the European Union has
also proposed a set of regulations across the board for artificial intelligence,
306 AIoT for smart transportation

including the “Artificial Intelligence Act,” which sorts AI applications by risk level
and applies more stringent requirements to the higher-risk systems. Autonomous
vehicle technology is among those high-risk systems.

  Moreover, member states of the European Union have their own sets of rules,
like the Road Traffic Act of Germany [31], that make it possible to implement
automated driving systems under certain conditions and contingent upon fulfilling
an authoritative set of mandates. In addition to their own regulations, EU member
states must also follow European directives. They can be quite detailed and cover
aspects of automobile operation that are crucial to the aspect of “safety.” In Japan,
the Road Transport Vehicle Act [20] is somewhat similar in that it has its own set of
legal requirements that must be satisfied before anything remotely resembling
automation can be put into practice and was revised to allow for the implementa-
tion of Level 3 autonomous automobiles on public roadways.

  China’s government [8] has introduced a variety of policies aimed at autono-
mous vehicles. One of the most impactful has been the “Guidelines on the
Establishment of the National Standards System for Autonomous Driving.” This
document covers much ground, but its main thrust is to introduce uniformity and
comparability into the national standard-setting process for AVs. Another key law
that applies to AVs is the newly minted Cybersecurity Law of the People’s
Republic of China. This law is quite important because it ensures that the large
amounts of data collected by these vehicles are secure.

  The costs and technological complexities of the infrastructure required for the
complete integration of artificial intelligence provide additional challenges in
addition to regulatory ones. For example, the impact and cost considerations of the
intelligent traffic signals or the communication networks that constitute the infra-
structure of AI in the transportation sector must be considered. Hence, the eva-
luation of the economic viability and cost-effectiveness of AI solutions in
transportation is essential. As necessary, it is facing the potential displacement of
workers due to this automation and figuring out how to retrain those workers.

  Technical challenges arise when using AI in diverse and complex environ-
ments. It is imperative to verify the precision and reliability of AIs forecasts and
assessments of security and effectiveness. Because data comes in all forms, from
incomplete to downright noisy, solving these technical problems demands smart,
sophisticated data management and processing.

  Finally, user acceptance and public perception play a crucial part in successfully
integrating AI into transportation. Ingraining AI into transportation involves technical
aspects and human ones, especially building public trust in AI-driven transportation
solutions. The push for the trust angle, which is very much part of the autonomous
vehicle dialogue, naturally leads to the need for a great understanding of the technology.

traffic lights, smart roads) will provide continuous data flow, which will be ana-
lyzed by AI to optimize routing, improve safety, and prevent accidents.

  AI evaluates the real-time data from sensors to recognize cars, pedestrians, and
barriers and decides whether to accelerate, brake, or steer. It will increase safety by
anticipating and responding to the possible threats, by which it reduces the number
of fatalities and accidents that are brought on by human mistakes. It also optimizes
the route and identifies the most effective routes by considering the environmental
factors and traffic patterns and by reducing fuel usage and delays.

15.3.2 Smart traffic management

By gathering and evaluating data from IoT-enabled sensors, cameras, and traffic
signals, AIoT will allow smart cities to optimize traffic flow. A few of the various
possibilities include dynamic traffic signal control to reduce congestion and AI that
instantly modifies traffic light timings. Sensor-equipped roads convey data for
prompt analysis and response by tracking traffic and weather, and intelligently
managing the road infrastructure. Predictive traffic modelling: AI forecasts traffic
and modifies routes based on previous and current data, saving commuters from
delays.

15.3.3 Enhanced public transport systems

AIoT will significantly transform and improve the efficiency and convenience of
public transportation through key innovations, which include real-time tracking.
IoT sensors provide live updates on vehicle locations and schedules, improving
passenger experience. Smart ticketing in AIoT will enable contactless and auto-
mated ticketing systems, such as mobile payments or biometric identification,
reducing queues and enhancing customer satisfaction. Further, in demand-
responsive transport, AI examines passenger demand patterns to optimize routes
and schedules, creating a flexible public transportation system that adapts to urban
needs and responds to fluctuating demand, particularly in cities.

15.3.4 Vehicle-to-everything (V2X) communication

V2X communication connects vehicles with their surroundings, which includes
other vehicles (V2V), infrastructure (V2I), and pedestrians (V2P). Powered by
316 AIoT for smart transportation

AIoT, it enables real-time exchange of data, allowing vehicles to communicate and
interact with traffic signals, road signs, and other vehicles to prevent accidents and
increase situational awareness, which include, for example, warnings about
hazards, blind spots, or sudden braking. Additionally, it also helps in improved
traffic coordination; smart infrastructure such as traffic lights and road sensors will
work in collaboration with vehicles to reduce congestion and optimize and improve
the traffic flow.

15.3.5 Predictive maintenance and fleet management

AIoT will revolutionize fleet management (for delivery, public transportation, or
logistics) with predictive maintenance that uses sensors to monitor vehicle com-
ponents (engine, tires, brakes, etc.). AI predicts failures by analyzing the data to
foresee issues before they happen, enabling proactive repairs and minimizing
downtime. Also, AI will analyze operational data to optimize vehicle usage,
scheduling, and fuel consumption, whereby reduces cost and improves efficiency.

15.3.6 Sustainability and energy efficiency

AIoT plays a crucial role in sustainable transportation by supporting electric
vehicles, where it manages charging infrastructure, optimizes energy usage,
extends battery life, and schedules accordingly. AIoT enhances efficiency by
identifying routes that support and ways by which fuel consumption is efficient by
identifying fuel-efficient routes and by optimizing driving strategies to reduce
emissions. Additionally, by incorporating carbon footprint monitoring, the AIoT
system keeps track of the environmental impact of vehicles and fleets, helping
cities and organizations to achieve their sustainability goals.

15.3.7 Personalized travel experience

AIoT enhances the user experience in transportation through highly personalized
smart apps that provide real-time travel updates to vehicles that adjust to indivi-
dual’s preferences through personalized navigation, where AI suggests optimal
routes based on habits, preferences, and real-time conditions by avoiding conges-
tion or accidents. To ensure passenger comfort, the system adjusts climate, lighting,
and seating to suit individual preferences, enhancing the overall travel experience.
Additionally, AI provides personalized travel recommendations by analyzing user
behaviour and preferences.

15.3.8 Integrated mobility ecosystems

The future of smart transportation with Mobility-as-a-Service will leverage AIoT
by integrating various modes of transportation (e.g., buses, bikes, shared vehicles,
and taxis) into a single service platform. AIoT will offer seamless connectivity
where it integrates different transportation models, and by analyzing travel data, it
reduces waiting times and the travel cost. It will also help in smart journey planning
where the users will be able to plan by seamlessly switching between different
modes of transport with real-time availability.

15.3.9 Enhanced safety and security

AIoT-driven safety measures play a crucial role in enhancing transportation safety
by preventing accidents, theft, and improving emergency responses through colli-
sion avoidance, where AI predicts and prevents accidents by analyzing the envir-
onment based on the equipment fitted in the vehicles. Emergency response
systems: AI can help detect accidents and provide real-time data to accelerate
emergency services, enabling faster response times. Security of the vehicle will
enable theft prevention by utilizing IoT sensors, GPS tracking, and surveillance
cameras.

15.3.10 AIoT for logistics and supply chain

AIoT transforms logistics and supply chain management through smart inventory
management where AI predicts demand and optimizes deliveries with IoT-
connected vehicles and warehouses. AIoT powers drones and self-driving trucks
for efficient and cost-effective deliveries. Monitoring of the shipments by real-time
tracking can be carried out, whereby reduces delays and enhances transparency.
